National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) — Guidelines and Action Framework
Source: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), India
Document ID: NCAP-2019-GUIDELINES

=============================================================
NATIONAL CLEAN AIR PROGRAMME (NCAP) — OVERVIEW
=============================================================

The National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) is a long-term, time-bound, national-level strategy to combat the air pollution problem across the country. It was launched in January 2019 with a target to achieve 20–30% reduction in PM2.5 and PM10 concentrations by 2024 (with 2017 as base year), later revised to 40% reduction by 2026.

Key Features:
- City-specific action plans for 131 non-attainment cities.
- Identification of key sectors contributing to pollution (vehicles, industries, dust, open burning).
- Multi-stakeholder engagement (ULBs, SPCBs, traffic police, industry).
- Monitoring and evaluation framework using CAAQMS data.

=============================================================
NCAP — SECTOR-WISE ACTION PLANS
=============================================================

1. VEHICULAR SECTOR
Targets:
- Transition to BS-VI vehicles and fuels.
- Phase out pre-BS-IV commercial vehicles from city roads.
- Strengthen PUC (Pollution Under Control) certification system.
- Promote electric vehicles (EVs): target 30% of new vehicle sales by 2030.
- BRTS (Bus Rapid Transit) and multi-modal connectivity to reduce personal vehicle use.

Actions for Cities:
- Designate low-emission zones in city centers.
- Restrict diesel taxis/autorickshaws; incentivize CNG/electric conversions.
- Deploy enforcement teams at key entry/exit points for PUC checking.
- Real-time vehicle count and emission estimates at major intersections.

2. INDUSTRIAL SECTOR
Targets:
- All major industries (thermal, cement, steel, brick kilns) to meet revised NAAQS stack emission standards.
- 100% monitoring via OCEMS (Online Continuous Emission Monitoring Systems) for large industries.
- Technology upgrades for highly polluting small/medium industries.

Actions for Cities:
- Annual inspection and renewal of Consent-to-Operate (CTO) for all industries.
- Immediate closure for industries operating without CTO or exceeding emission norms.
- Stack emission testing of all industries in non-attainment cities at least once per year.
- Brick kilns: mandatory adoption of zigzag technology; ban on traditional fixed-chimney kilns.

3. DUST CONTROL (CONSTRUCTION AND ROADS)
Targets:
- Zero visible dust from construction sites and roads in the top 50 non-attainment cities.
- 100% mechanized sweeping of arterial roads in non-attainment cities.

Actions for Cities:
- Registration of all construction sites ≥500 sq.m on the CPCB/CAQM portal.
- Mandatory installation of anti-smog guns at sites ≥20,000 sq.m.
- Water sprinklers at construction site boundaries; covered material storage.
- Road-side dust suppression: daily water sprinkling, green belts, road side drains.
- Capture and reuse of C&D (Construction and Demolition) waste at dedicated processing plants.

4. OPEN BURNING
Targets:
- Zero instances of open burning of garbage, leaves, agricultural waste, and municipal solid waste.
- Complete transition from open dumping to sanitary landfills or waste-to-energy.

Actions for Cities:
- Deployment of satellite-based fire detection (MODIS/VIIRS) for real-time monitoring.
- Integration of fire alerts into enforcement systems for immediate ground action.
- Awareness campaigns for farmers: crop residue management alternatives.
- SHG involvement in collection and composting of agricultural residue.

5. BIOMASS AND HOUSEHOLD BURNING
Targets:
- Transition of 5 crore households from biomass cooking to LPG/PNG by 2024.
- Elimination of municipal waste burning in urban areas.

Actions for Cities:
- Ujjwala Yojana beneficiary tracking and refill support.
- Community composting programs for organic waste.
- Enforcement of Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016.

=============================================================
NCAP — ENFORCEMENT AND MONITORING FRAMEWORK
=============================================================

1. Real-Time Monitoring
- Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS): 1000+ stations across India.
- Data published on CPCB CAMS portal in near-real-time.
- City-level AQI bulletin published daily by CPCB.

2. Performance Review
- Quarterly review of city action plan progress by CPCB.
- Annual ranking of NCAP cities based on PM2.5 reduction.
- Cities achieving targets: eligible for Smart Cities and AMRUT funding.
- Cities not achieving targets: enhanced scrutiny; possible suspension of grants.

3. Citizen Grievance
- NCAP grievance portal for citizens to report violations.
- 24-hour control rooms in each non-attainment city.
- Integration with national Clean Air Helpline.

=============================================================
FUNDING AND INCENTIVES
=============================================================

- 15th Finance Commission grants: Cities earmark 25% for air quality.
- NCAP grants to non-attainment cities: ₹100–500 crore over 5 years based on population.
- Green Finance: MoEFCC channels World Bank climate funds for EV infrastructure, clean cooking, industrial upgrades.

=============================================================
LEGAL AND REGULATORY BASIS
=============================================================

1. Environment Protection Act, 1986 — Primary authority for national air quality standards.
2. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 — SPCB establishment and powers.
3. National Environmental Policy, 2006 — Framework for pollution control as national priority.
4. NCAP Notification, 2019 — MoEFCC gazette notification establishing NCAP framework.
5. NGT Orders on Non-Attainment Cities — Judicial oversight of NCAP implementation progress.

=============================================================
SPECIFIC ACTIONS FOR ENFORCEMENT AGENTS IN NON-ATTAINMENT CITIES
=============================================================

Priority 1: High-Contribution Sources (Based on Local Source Apportionment)
- Identify top 5 source categories contributing >60% of PM2.5 locally.
- Concentrate 80% of enforcement resources on those source categories.
- Document inspections with GPS-tagged photographs and meter readings.

Priority 2: Seasonal Compliance Drives
- Winter (October–February): Focus on open burning, industrial stack emissions, vehicular emissions.
- Summer (March–June): Focus on road dust, construction sites.
- Monsoon (July–September): Focus on industrial effluent and waste management.

Priority 3: Hotspot Management
- Identify top 10 hotspot areas (highest AQI cells) using CAAQMS + model data.
- Deploy dedicated enforcement teams to each hotspot for 30-day intensive campaigns.
- Track AQI reduction post-enforcement to measure effectiveness.

Priority 4: Inter-Agency Coordination
- Monthly cross-agency review meetings (SPCB + Traffic + Municipal + Industries Dept).
- Shared enforcement data on city pollution control platform.
- Joint raids for complex violations (e.g., illegal brick kilns with vehicular transport).
